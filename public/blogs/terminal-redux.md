# Terminal Redux and my comeuppance

I recently had the pleasure of listening to the thrash metal album "Terminal Redux" and all I could think of was how I have not heard of a concept album with space opera theme before. The concept itself was itching and gnawing at me, and that was satiated by Vektor with this beautiful album.

## Metal and concept albums

I generally am not one to search for new music - once I find a groove, I stick with it for quite some time (for months even). Certain phases I've had in the past include deathcore, metalcore, rock, progressive metal and djent recently with Meshuggah. Metal for me isn't just about the riffs, drumming or cool things like solos or breakdowns, but its lyricism too, as it gives any song just that much more character and meaning. (personal opinion alert)

Concept albums like Still Life, Metropolis Pt. 2, Crack the Skye, (Catch 33?), From Mars to Sirius, and god knows how many more are great and bring a lot to the table, yet they somehow weren't something I was particularly looking for. It started with Gojira's "Space and Time" when I first heard how astronomical concepts can be described vividly and so hauntingly through metal. Ironic how the "loudest" music complements the audible deafness of empty space. From Mars to Sirius was the next step I suppose for me, Gojira offered a lot through the album but it was far more abstract than a story.

## From Mars to Sirius: A nice thought

This album upon first listen only had a couple of good listens that I would revisit (like "Flying Whales"), however I think it does offer a decent story albeit an abstract one at that. In my opinion, it involves in the us finding creatures to fly through space and eventually finding a way to move from war (mars) to peace (sirius) and finding answers, before making the big journey I suppose (I don't want to do a full breakdown of the album here, so apologies for the oversimplification).

While being a nice thought with great songs, it didnt quite tickle my expected fancy of a grandiose space opera.

## Terminal Redux: My thoughts vindicated

Just finished my first whole album listen of this today and I HAD to write this as it was an incredible experience. If you would like the full story the album tells, here you go: [heavyblogisheavy link](https://www.heavyblogisheavy.com/2016/08/19/prognotes-vektors-terminal-redux/). In terms of the actual music itself I would say it is quite refreshing for me as I have been listening to djent and adjacent subgenre for the better part of a year so a thrash concept album was very welcome to my ears. But it didn't feel like Slayer, Metallica or Sepultura (and any others I have listened to). The lyrics made that blaringly obvious that it was telling me an interesting story.

Even in thrash metal music I have heard so far, I hadn't listened to so many clean vocal sections and "progressive" elements (like a calm bridge or instrumentals) before and that blended so well with how the story progressed on (no spoilers!). The latter part of the album is just chef's kiss that concludes a Dune like saga and the fallacy of human curiosity and ego. The protagonist (singer) is someone you don't necessary root for morally, but you just can't wait to watch what he does as the album progresses. Some nice things to note is that the harsh vocals were quite audible for me and some use of a choir in some songs and great metaphors used throughout the album.

With all that said, I was blown away by the album mainly due to it finally revealing me a side of metal I was waiting to listen to: space opera and its derivatives. 

### Terminal Redux: Some highlights

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> Spoilers below!
>

- "Charging the Void" is such a good song - the section when "Iso Subject 5" becomes immortal from "Alshain" with the vocalization in the background with this ethereal feeling, actually feels like he has become a god, after drifting in the "great divide" for however long he was stranded in. He feels like a heir apparent to ascend a throne no human could sit on, it was very cool to imagine.

Once you listen to this track after listening to "Recharging the Void", there are so many parallels to both the tracks (same vocalization is then used for a more morbid scene)

- The instrumental song "Mountains Above the Sun" is a nice little break from the blasting music, it gave some breathing room to deep in the story and what is going currently: at this time "Iso Subject 5" has become the leader of "Cygnus" human society and is in the process of forever changing how a human lives and perceives life (through LCD and Pteropticon).

- The last 2 songs felt so perfect ("Collapse" and "Recharging the Void") with so many emotions and cool sections in both songs (parallels as mentioned in the first point), one thing I really noticed was the juxtaposition of feeling alive as "Iso Subject 5" was charging "Cygnus Terminal" and humanity to "Alshain" (leading to their demise) to conclude this forever still lives of man (check the blog for a better deep dive and context), thus the album's name "Terminal Redux". That last track felt so alive and eerily cheerful (not sure how else to put it), the vocalization felt like angels cheering us and "Iso" to take the leap and "give his flesh back to Shahin-i-tarazu", a full circle from the start of the album.

- There were certain parts of David DiSanto's harsh vocals I really liked (like some shrieks and ofc the clarity of his words as he sang)

- Surprising number of clean vocal sections, definitely didn't expect it, but they are always welcome.

There is still A LOT of symbolism and story I didn't touch on, I highly recommend you check the album out for yourself, even if you aren't a metal fan. The best thing for me to focus on was how "Iso Subject 5" developed throughout the album (how he felt like a god, only to be the cause for the "Cygnus" empire's demise)

## Comeuppance?

My punishment or a negative consequence of listening to this album is that urge to reinvigorate myself to start writing more (poems or short story - not fixated on anything yet). Why is this a punishment? I feel that the best thoughts come out naturally and not through "immediate" stimulation from media like movies, shows or music. It leads to a distorted opinion that what I conceived was unique and a tangent from the original source material, but in reality it feels more like a spin-off or a "food-for-thought". Upon reviewing the drafts and editing you realize the glaring correlation, and in the effort of staying original, you start to make heavy revisions that do not resemble the draft at all. Note that I use the word "immediate" ergo some AV content I have consumed very recently that moved me enough to try something that rivals what I witnessed. Naturally, any piece of human work, literary or otherwise, is derived from some past work. However, the difference between active research and active day-dreaming becomes apparent for me atleast. The stories I come up with naturally vs when I have gained temporary inspiration from recent media are vastly different in quality. I hope to not make the same mistake with "Terminal Redux", I'll try to simmer my thoughts down and let it live with me for a while before I use this new found enthusiasm to write more.
